# Source Preset Classification

> Created: 2026-06-20
>
> Purpose: define how MajorRSS should separate broad official presets from vertical source packs.

## Product Positioning

The official preset library should start with broad usefulness, not maximum vertical precision.

A user who enables only the official baseline should still receive useful information across:

- world news
- economy and markets
- politics and policy
- public health and safety
- technology
- major institutional announcements

This baseline may be less vertical, less specialized, and sometimes less timely than expert packs. That is acceptable. Its job is to prevent an empty or narrow first-run experience.

Vertical packs are a second layer. They should deepen coverage for specific domains:

- Technology
- Economy and markets
- Politics and policy
- Healthcare and medicine
- Academia and papers
- AI and frontier labs
- Security
- Crypto / Web3
- Open source and developer tools

## Layer Model

### 1. General Baseline

Broad, durable, official or highly authoritative sources.

Examples:

- BBC
- Guardian
- Al Jazeera
- Deutsche Welle
- France 24
- NPR
- New York Times
- Wall Street Journal
- Financial Times
- Bloomberg
- NHK
- Japan Times
- South China Morning Post
- Channel NewsAsia
- ABC Australia
- CBC
- The Hindu
- Le Monde
- ANSA
- Arab News
- AllAfrica
- Agencia Brasil
- Buenos Aires Times
- Mexico News Daily
- Taipei Times
- Xinhua / Xinhuanet
- Reuters candidate
- Associated Press candidate

Selection rule:

```text
If this is the only collection a new user enables, the feed should still feel alive and useful.
```

Bias-control rule:

```text
The general baseline should not be dominated by one country, one language region, or one editorial tradition.
```

This does not mean every region has equal source quality or RSS support. It means the baseline should intentionally include different national and regional viewpoints when stable public feeds are available.

### 2. Vertical Packs

Domain-specific sources with stronger signal for a field.

Examples:

- AI: OpenAI, DeepMind, Mistral, Anthropic, Claude, Meta AI, arXiv AI categories.
- Developer tools: GitHub Changelog, Vercel, Cloudflare, Supabase, SDK release feeds.
- Economy: central banks, SEC, Bloomberg markets, WSJ markets, FT, public investment-bank and asset-manager insights.
- Policy: Federal Register, CISA, NIST, central bank speeches and press releases.
- Healthcare: WHO, CDC, FDA, NIH, major journal feeds.
- Academia: arXiv, Nature, Science, PubMed query feeds, institution blogs.
- Crypto / Web3: Bitcoin, protocol foundations, exchanges, chain analytics, compliance, smart-contract security, and relevant regulators.

Selection rule:

```text
The source should improve depth, timeliness, or authority for a specific domain.
```

### 3. High-weight Non-RSS Sources

Not all valuable sources are RSS.

MajorRSS should support non-RSS sources in vertical packs when they are high-value:

- founder / CEO / researcher social accounts
- official product changelog pages
- standards pages
- regulator pages
- high-signal newsletters
- GitHub releases, commits, discussions, or issues
- public status pages
- pages monitored by diff

These should not be hidden as fake RSS. Mark them by `source_type`:

- `web_page`
- `page_diff`
- `account`
- `api`
- `github_release`
- `rsshub`

### 4. People Radar

High-signal people should be represented as source presets too.

Examples:

- AI researchers and lab leaders: Andrej Karpathy, Sam Altman, Dario Amodei, Fei-Fei Li, Yann LeCun, Demis Hassabis, Andrew Ng.
- Technology founders: Elon Musk, Sam Altman, Brian Armstrong.
- Crypto/Web3 people: Vitalik Buterin, Brian Armstrong.

These sources should use `source_type = account` when they are social accounts, or `source_type = web_page` when the useful source is a personal essay page.

They must be labeled separately from official institution sources:

```text
person_account
person_page
```

Pure RSS mode behavior:

```text
If a user subscribes to a people radar collection without task-level filters, the app should show whatever those people publish in the raw feed.
```

This is expected. Filtering belongs to the user's task configuration:

- keep keywords;
- ignore keywords;
- selected collections;
- selected accounts;
- AI semantic relevance in BYOK mode.

People radar collections should therefore be optional subpacks, not silently bundled into every official baseline.

## Trust Labels

Every source should expose its trust boundary:

- `official_feed`: publisher provides RSS or Atom directly.
- `official_page`: publisher provides the page, but not RSS.
- `web_page_candidate`: valuable page, not yet converted or monitored.
- `third_party_generated`: RSS generated by an external project from public pages.
- `generated_by_majorss`: RSS generated by MajorRSS from public pages.
- `person_account`: high-signal personal account.
- `person_page`: high-signal personal website or essay page.

This is important for sources such as Reuters, AP, Anthropic, Claude, and Meta AI where the canonical source may be official, but RSS availability varies.

## Current Seed Direction

`docs/source_presets.seed.json` now contains:

- `general_baseline`: broad world/business/politics coverage.
- `regional_perspectives_baseline`: cross-country mainstream feeds used to reduce single-region bias.
- `market_and_economy_baseline`: broad finance and macro coverage.
- `financial_institutions_research`: public investment-bank and asset-manager insights with stricter content-rights boundaries.
- `healthcare_medicine`: public health, medical regulation, and biomedical research.
- `academic_research`: broad academic and scientific sources.
- `policy_international_orgs`: government and international organization feeds.
- `cybersecurity_watch`: vulnerabilities, threat intelligence, incidents, and security policy.
- `crypto_web3_watch`: crypto assets, blockchain infrastructure, Web3 security, exchanges, and compliance.
- `ai_people_radar`, `tech_founders_people_radar`, and `crypto_people_radar`: optional high-signal people source packs.
- existing vertical packs for AI, developer tools, papers, regulatory radar, and service status.

The next source-gathering pass should refine vertical depth in this order:

1. Add healthcare subpacks for rare disease, drug approvals, clinical trials, and patient organizations.
2. Add politics subpacks by region and institution.
3. Add economy subpacks for central banks, banking regulation, institutional research, commodities, and company filings.
4. Add crypto/Web3 subpacks for Bitcoin, DeFi, stablecoins, exchanges, custody, and on-chain security.
5. Add technology subpacks for semiconductors, cloud, security, AI infrastructure, and open source.
6. Add academia subpacks by discipline with PubMed / arXiv query feeds.
7. Add high-weight non-RSS accounts for founders, CEOs, researchers, regulators, and institutions.

## Promotion Rules

A source can move toward built-in recommendation when:

- it is public and does not require auth;
- it has stable URL identity;
- it passes feed or page health checks;
- it has clear canonical ownership;
- it has acceptable copyright and redistribution boundaries;
- it has a known noise profile;
- its category role is clear.

Candidate sources should remain visible but labeled until they pass these checks.
